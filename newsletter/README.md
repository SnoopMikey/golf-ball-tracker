# The Monthly Dispatch — production guide

A monthly email recapping the previous month's ball finds. Mike sends it himself
from an Airtable automation. The copy is **written fresh each month by Claude** —
it is not generated by a script, and it should not read like it was.

`2026-07/` is the reference issue. Copy its `index.html` and rewrite the content;
that guarantees the format stays identical month to month.

---

## Making an issue

```bash
python3 scripts/newsletter_prep.py 2026-08
```

That exports the photos at the right sizes and crops into `newsletter/2026-08/img/`,
prints a data brief (counts, brands, conditions, weather, doubleheaders, extremes),
and writes `_contact-sheet.jpg`.

**Then actually look at the contact sheet.** Every good line in the July issue came
from looking at the photos, not the data: Mike's shadow with a companion beside him,
a mower scar across a Titleist, a traffic-cone orange Callaway lying against a red
curb. The numbers give you the skeleton; the pictures give you the issue.

Then:

1. `cp newsletter/2026-07/index.html newsletter/2026-08/index.html` and rewrite.
2. Update every image URL to the new month's folder.
3. Commit and push — **images must be live before the email is sent.**
4. Create a row in the Airtable `Newsletters` table (`tblwsJW3WxJjG0F7n`) with
   Issue, Subject, Preheader, Web Version and Status = `Ready to Send`.
5. `python3 scripts/newsletter_publish.py 2026-08` — converts the issue into an
   email-safe fragment and writes it to that row's HTML field. **Do not paste
   `index.html` into Airtable directly; it will arrive mangled.** See below.
6. Mike's automation picks it up from there.

Preview a finished issue the way a recipient sees it:

```bash
"/Applications/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome" --headless \
  --screenshot=out.png --window-size=700,5200 https://mikesballs.com/newsletter/2026-08/
```

## Structure (keep this order)

1. **Masthead** — `logo.png` on ink green, "THE MONTHLY DISPATCH" beneath
2. **Issue line** — `No. 00N ❦ Month Year ❦ View in browser`
3. **Headline + dek** — two lines, the dek is a fragment list
4. **Opening** — two paragraphs: the month in context, then what follows
5. **The Scorecard** — six stats, 3×2
6. **Ball of the Month** — the standout, ~300px wide, centered, with a paragraph
7. **The Rest of the Field** — 2-column grid of square crops, newest first,
   each with number, date, brand · condition, and a short wry caption
8. **Notes from the Field** — 4–6 bolded observations, the heart of the issue
9. **Weather Endured** — three stats and a paragraph
10. **CTA** — "Rate the Balls" → mikesballs.com/#list
11. **Sign-off** — `hero.png` and a one-line farewell
12. **Footer** — ink green, site link, no unsubscribe

## Voice

Same tongue-in-cheek register as the site, dialed **slightly higher**. The joke is
that a faintly absurd subject is documented with total institutional seriousness —
"the only documented instance of his balls being witnessed in the field." Play the
double meanings straight and never wink twice at the same one. Keep it suggestive,
never crude.

Reliable devices: understatement ("a closing statement if ever there was one"),
mock-archival authority ("there was no official record for doubleheaders before
this month"), and treating lost balls as having had careers.

**House rules**

- American spelling and imperial units (miles, °F, mph).
- Never remark on finds landing on a weekend — Mike almost always hunts weekends,
  so it is not a pattern worth noting.
- No unsubscribe link. Mike adds people manually; the footer just tells them to
  ask him.
- Every stat must come from the data. Do not round a fact into a better joke.
- Describe only what is actually visible in a photo. If you cannot tell what
  something is, say so or leave it out — do not guess and assert.

## Why there is a publish step

The first July send arrived as a wall of escaped markup. Airtable sanitises
whatever goes through an automation's email body, and it does three things that
break a hand-written HTML email:

| What Airtable does | Consequence | Handled by |
|---|---|---|
| Turns every **newline** into `<p><span>…</span></p>` | Tables are sliced apart — an opening `<tr>` is auto-closed at end of line and its cells become siblings instead of children | emitting the whole email on **one line** |
| Escapes `<html>`, `<head>`, `<meta>`, `<title>`, `<body>` and **HTML comments** | They render as literal text the reader sees | sending a **fragment** with comments stripped |
| Strips `width`, `align`, `valign`, `cellpadding`, `cellspacing`, `border` from `<table>`/`<td>` (but keeps `style`, and keeps `width`/`alt` on `<img>`) | Column widths and centering collapse | rewriting layout attributes as **inline CSS** |

`scripts/newsletter_publish.py` does all three and pushes the result to Airtable.
It leaves `index.html` alone — that stays readable and is what the web version
serves. The generated `email.html` sits beside it.

Note that a fixed-width `<table>` is block-level, so `text-align:center` on its
parent cell will not center it; it needs `margin-left:auto;margin-right:auto`.
The script adds that automatically.

To check a build before sending, strip the same attributes Airtable strips and
render it — if it still looks right, the inline CSS is genuinely carrying the
layout.

## Technical constraints

- **Airtable attachment URLs expire in roughly a day** (the expiry is baked into
  the URL). Newsletter images must be exported and committed here, never hot-linked.
- Use absolute `https://mikesballs.com/...` URLs. Relative paths break in email.
- Tables for layout, inline styles only, 600px body — no `<style>` blocks, no flexbox.
- Gmail strips webfonts, so the email uses a Georgia stack rather than the site's
  Fraunces, and clips messages over ~102KB of HTML (an issue runs ~24KB).
- Every image needs `width`, `alt`, and `display:block`.
- Airtable long-text fields cap at 100,000 characters.
