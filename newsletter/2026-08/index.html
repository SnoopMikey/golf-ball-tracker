Fewer Balls,
Heavier Air

Seven recovered, and four of them scraped together the hard way. A month twelve points muggier than July. The fiftieth ball in the register. And, for the first time, an audience with opinions.

Seven balls sounds like a respectable follow‑up to July’s record eight. The number flatters the month. Nothing came home at all until the 15th — twenty‑seven days without a single ball, the longest dry spell of the year. Then on the morning of the 16th, three turned up inside ten minutes. Set that one purple patch aside and August produced four balls in thirty‑one days. It was, by any honest reading, a light month.

It was also a heavy one. The air sat at seventy‑two percent humidity on average and the wind never once rose above four miles an hour, which is a combination Mike walked into five separate times without complaint. And on the 29th, in a gutter full of dead leaves, the register turned over to fifty.

—  Ball of the Month  —

No. 047 — Top Flite, Mint

August 16  ·  9:26 AM  ·  73°F, clear, 3 mph

Highlighter green, unmarked, and lying flush against the bottom of a chain‑link fence in grass of very nearly the same shade. A ball this color is manufactured on the theory that it cannot be lost. Somebody lost it anyway, and then walked past a fence line it was sitting under in plain view. It is the only Mint specimen of the month, the fourth in the register’s history, and — at the risk of playing favorites — the most cheerful thing Mike has ever pulled out of the weeds. It was also the third ball he picked up in ten minutes, which is not a bad way to end a run.

The archive takes no editorial position on any ball in its custody, but it will note that the same reader who called one specimen absolute perfection gave another a single star and sent it to paradise. That is the range this operation now works in.

Fifty. No. 050 came up on the morning of the 29th, out of a gutter packed with dead leaves at the corner of a crosswalk — a scuffed TaylorMade with dirt worked into the dimples and one small green streak under the number. Fifty balls into this undertaking and the round number went to a ball nobody would have chosen for the honor. The register does not take requests.

Three in ten minutes. The 16th accounted for nearly half the month on its own: 9:16, 9:18, and 9:26. The first two were roughly fifty feet apart, which is less a hunt than a harvest; the third came a quarter‑mile up the road and happens to be the finest ball of the month. It is the third triple‑header on record, and the first to end in a Mint.

Titleist deposed. July belonged to Titleist — four balls of eight. August handed over exactly one. TaylorMade took three and the month with it, turning up on the 15th, the 23rd and the 29th like a brand with a schedule to keep. Srixon took two, one of them a range ball that is currently the lowest‑rated object in the collection.

Twenty‑seven days of nothing. The gap between No. 043 on July 19 and No. 044 on August 15 is the longest this year and the sixth longest ever. It ended in leaf litter on top of a curb, under the only gray sky of the month: a TaylorMade in Great condition, marked with orange chevrons, invisible in a drift of dead brown leaves. Announcing yourself in orange only works where there is nothing else orange lying around.

Strongest Breeze

A close, sticky, unrewarding month to be outdoors. Five of the seven recoveries happened in humidity of seventy percent or higher; in July, not one of eight did. The average climbed twelve points and the air never moved — the strongest gust recorded all month was four miles an hour, and three of the five outings managed three or less. The 23rd was the hot one at 81°F. The muggiest was the 15th at seventy‑nine percent, which was also the coolest morning and the only overcast one, because the weather is not obliged to be consistent about how uncomfortable it makes things. Six of seven balls came home under clear skies and every one of them came home warm.

Three of you have now gone on the record.
Fifty balls are waiting, and forty‑seven of them have never been judged at all.
