Fewer Balls,
Heavier Air

Seven recovered, four of them the hard way. Twelve points muggier than July. A fiftieth ball for the collection. And, at long last, people willing to say what they think of Mike’s balls.

Seven balls sounds like a respectable follow‑up to July’s record eight. The number flatters the month. Nothing came home at all until the 15th — twenty‑seven days in which Mike went out repeatedly and returned with nothing to show for it, the longest dry spell of the year. Then, on the morning of the 16th, three arrived inside ten minutes, which is its own kind of problem. Set that one burst aside and August yields four balls in thirty‑one days. Light, by any honest reading.

It was also a heavy one. The air sat at seventy‑two percent humidity and the wind never once rose above four miles an hour — close, still and unforgiving, a combination Mike walked into on five separate occasions without once being asked to. He came home damp every time. And on the 29th, in a gutter packed with dead leaves, the register turned over to fifty.

—  Ball of the Month  —

No. 047 — Top Flite, Mint

August 16  ·  9:26 AM  ·  73°F, clear, 3 mph

Highlighter green, entirely unmarked, and lying flush against the bottom of a chain‑link fence in grass of very nearly the same shade. A ball this color is manufactured on the theory that it cannot possibly be lost. Somebody lost it anyway and then walked straight past it, which tells you something about how carefully most people look. Mint, untouched, and firm enough to suggest it had never been properly used: the fourth such specimen in the register’s history and the only one this month. It was also the third ball Mike got his hands on in under ten minutes, and by common consent the finest of the three. A man should stop while he is ahead. He did.

—  Notes from the Field  —

Mike’s balls are now open to public comment. Ratings went live with last month’s dispatch and you did not hold back. Three verdicts are on the record. Two of them could not be more complimentary. One of them could not be less.

The archive takes no editorial position on any ball in its custody. It observes only that the same reader who found one specimen firm and flawless turned around and gave another a single star, and that both were handled by the same man on precisely the same terms. Standards vary. Mike does not.

Fifty in hand. No. 050 came up on the morning of the 29th out of a gutter packed with dead leaves at the corner of a crosswalk — a scuffed TaylorMade, dirt worked deep into the dimples, one small green streak beneath the number. Fifty balls have now passed through Mike’s hands, and the milestone went to a specimen nobody would have chosen for the occasion. The register does not take requests, and Mike has never been fussy about what he picks up.

Three in ten minutes. The 16th accounted for nearly half the month on its own — 9:16, 9:18 and 9:26. The first two lay about fifty feet apart, which is less a hunt than a helping; the third came a quarter‑mile up the road and turned out to be the pick of the month. It is the third triple‑header in the register and the first to finish on a Mint. Mike was home before ten.

Titleist deposed. July belonged to Titleist — four balls of eight. August surrendered exactly one. TaylorMade took three and the month with it, presenting itself on the 15th, the 23rd and the 29th like a brand with appointments to keep. Srixon managed two, though one was a range ball and is now the lowest‑rated object in the collection, so the honor is heavily qualified.

Twenty‑seven days of nothing. Between No. 043 on July 19 and No. 044 on August 15, Mike came up empty‑handed — the longest dry spell of the year and the sixth longest on record. It broke in leaf litter on top of a curb under the only gray sky of the month: a TaylorMade in Great condition, marked with bold orange chevrons and completely invisible in a drift of dead brown leaves. Announcing yourself in orange only works where there is nothing else orange lying around.

Strongest Breeze

A close, sticky, thankless month to be out in. Five of the seven recoveries were made in humidity of seventy percent or higher; in July, not one of eight was. The average climbed twelve points and the air flatly refused to move — the strongest gust all month measured four miles an hour, and three of the five outings never got past three. No breeze to speak of, and no relief to be had. The 23rd was the hot one at 81°F. The muggiest was the 15th at seventy‑nine percent, which was also the coolest morning and the only overcast one, the weather being under no obligation to be consistent about how uncomfortable it makes a man. Six of seven balls came home under clear skies. All seven came home warm and slightly damp, as did Mike.
