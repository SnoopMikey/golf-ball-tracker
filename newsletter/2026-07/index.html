A Record Month
for Mike's Balls

Eight recovered. Two doubleheaders. One immaculate Srixon. Comfortably Mike's biggest handful to date.

July was, by the numbers, the finest month in the history of this operation. Eight balls came home — matching May for the all‑time record and improving on June by a full sixty percent. Mike worked six separate mornings, doubled up on two of them, and closed the month holding the best specimen he has handled all year.

Forty‑three balls have now passed through his hands. He remembers every one of them. What follows is the complete accounting: every ball, where it was lying, and what the sky was doing at the moment of recovery.

—  Ball of the Month  —

No. 043 — Srixon, Mint

July 19  ·  9:42 AM  ·  76°F, clear, 1 mph

The only Mint ball of the month, and the last one Mike bagged before the calendar turned — a closing statement if ever there was one. It was sitting in ankle‑deep grass looking essentially factory fresh: no scuffs, no grass burn, the green script still crisp enough to read from a standing position. Somebody lost this ball very early in its career and never came back for it. Of the forty‑three balls Mike has handled, this is comfortably the finest.

—  Notes from the Field  —

Mike brought a friend. Look closely at No. 040 and you will count two silhouettes stretched across the pavement: the man himself, ball held aloft like a small trophy, and beside him a rare companion along for the morning. Forty‑three entries into this archive it is the first appearance by anyone other than Mike, and the only documented instance of his balls being witnessed in the field.

Two for one, twice over. On the 11th and again on the 12th, Mike went out and came back with a pair. The 11th covered nearly a kilometre in twenty minutes; the 12th was a more leisurely half‑kilometre. There was no official record for doubleheaders before this month. There is now, and it stands at two.

A ball of a different colour. No. 038 turned up in traffic‑cone orange — the only ball all month with the confidence to announce itself — lying beside a freshly painted red curb. Whether that constitutes terrible camouflage or excellent camouflage depends entirely on your feelings about red.

Honest wear. No. 039 came in with a green scar clean across its equator, the sort of mark a mower leaves behind. Graded Worn, and the grade is doing it no favours and telling no lies.

Titleist country. Half of July's haul carried the Titleist script — four of eight. Srixon took two, Callaway and Marathon one apiece. The Marathon, a humble range ball, turned up on the Fourth of July, which is surely the correct brand for the occasion.

A generous month. Only one of the eight recoveries happened under genuine overcast — against a career rate of forty percent, July was practically Mediterranean. The lone afternoon shift, on the 18th, topped out at 81°F with a six‑mile‑an‑hour breeze: the warmest ball handled all month, and the only one Mike collected after lunch.
